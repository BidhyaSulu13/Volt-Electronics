# Volt storefront design research

**Project category:** Multi-category electronics retailer for shoppers in Nepal. The site supports product discovery, comparison, wishlists, and a sample cart. Prices, offers, testimonials, and fulfilment claims are demonstration content.

**Research date:** 24 November 2026

## References reviewed

| Reference | Observations from the live site | Design direction taken for Volt |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Aasma Stores](https://www.aasmastores.com/) | Product-led rotating campaign banners, clear category entry points, selected product collections, customer feedback, and newsletter/footer information. | Keep the homepage journey easy to scan: campaign, categories, products, then support content. Use an original electronics art direction and copy. |
| [Apple](https://www.apple.com/) | Large, focused product campaigns with short headlines, strong product imagery, and a small number of clear actions. Product discovery is organized by device family. | Give each hero slide one message and one category action. Make product families easy to find. |
| [Samsung](https://www.samsung.com/us/) | Product campaigns are paired with category navigation, model comparison, promotional collections, and clear "learn" versus "buy" actions. | Keep browsing and buying actions distinct, and make category filtering available from the shop. |
| [Bang & Olufsen](https://www.bang-olufsen.com/en/us) | Editorial photography and restrained copy make the physical product the focus. Product storytelling is balanced with service and support information. | Use a calmer image treatment and give product images room; retain practical delivery and help details lower on the page. |
| [Nothing](https://nothing.tech/) | A distinctive product identity carries through category links, launch stories, product features, and straightforward shop calls to action. | Keep a consistent Volt identity while varying section layouts and imagery; avoid repeating the same product grid for every story. |
| [Sonos](https://www.sonos.com/en/home) | Campaign imagery leads into specific offers, product groupings, service benefits, and product education. | Pair promotional content with a direct route to relevant products and explain sample offer status clearly. |
| [Bose](https://www.bose.com/home) | The page combines a product-led campaign with recognizable product cards, prices, ratings, and purchase actions. | Keep price, discount, rating, and add-to-cart information legible and consistent in product cards. |
| [Dyson](https://www.dyson.com.au/) | Category navigation is organized around clear product families, with support and product-selection tools discoverable from the main navigation. | Keep Volt's categories, filters, and contact/support routes visible and understandable. |

## Art direction and implementation

- **Palette:** warm off-white surfaces, deep charcoal text, soft sage/stone panels, and a restrained Volt lime accent for primary actions and selected states.
- **Typography:** DM Sans for readable product and interface text, Manrope for display headings, and DM Mono only for short technical labels. Avoid tiny all-caps copy for essential information.
- **Composition:** keep the full-width campaign carousel as the main entry point, then alternate compact category and product layouts with a small number of editorial/promotional panels.
- **Product presentation:** consistent crop proportions, visible product names and NPR prices, clear discount treatment, accessible controls, and explicit sample-data notes.
- **Motion:** use short opacity/position reveals on scroll, controlled hover transitions, and a subtle carousel image transition. Respect reduced-motion preferences; no added animation dependency.
- **Responsive behavior:** preserve a useful campaign crop and readable actions on mobile, allow category/product grids to collapse cleanly, and maintain touch-sized controls.

Reference sites are research only. Volt does not reuse their logos, copy, or proprietary assets.
